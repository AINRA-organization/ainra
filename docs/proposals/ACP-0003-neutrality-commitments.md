## What it proposes

AINRA aims to be the one shared identity root of Web4 ([../WEB4.md](../WEB4.md)). One root is what the era needs and
exactly what platforms and regulators will resist if it behaves like a monopolist. So AINRA commits, in its rules:

1. Checking a passport or a link is free and works offline, forever.
2. The root never sets or recommends what registrars charge. Its own fees are published, capped, and never changed for
   work already done.
3. Accreditation follows published, objective criteria; it is never exclusive; refusals give reasons and can be
   appealed; a registrar may also join any other root.
4. AINRA earns its place as a default in open code. It never pays for placement and never asks for exclusivity.
5. An agent survives its registrar: registrars keep escrowed records, and a lineage can move (D-102).
6. Open-source and individual agents have a path that costs nothing at the level that admits existence (L0).
7. "Web4" and "Web 4.0" are never claimed as trade marks.
8. When a legal entity exists, its assets carry a lock: no sale or conversion of the root, and a named public-interest
   successor.

## Why

Shared roots that became universal stayed out of the market they enabled, kept checking free, and won defaults in
open code; the ones taken to court set prices, wrote exclusive membership rules, or changed fees backwards. Non-profit
form has never exempted anyone; conduct is judged. The research is summarised in D-115.
